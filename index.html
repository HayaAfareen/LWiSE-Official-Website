Who We Are

A community, not just a society.

LWiSE is a student-led society at the Lahore University of Management Sciences (LUMS) dedicated to empowering women in science, technology, engineering, mathematics, research, and academia.

We believe the problem is not a lack of talent. It's a lack of access — to opportunities, mentors, research, resources, community, and representation. LWiSE exists to build those bridges.

What We Do

Five pillars. One mission.

From workshops to research competitions, from mentorship to outreach — everything we do is designed to create one thing: pathways.

🔬
Research & Academia

Research competitions, scientific writing workshops, faculty connections, academic resources, and opportunities to publish. We make research feel reachable.

🗺️
Opportunities

Curated internships, scholarships, fellowships, competitions, and courses. If an opportunity exists for women in STEM, we share it.

🤝
Mentorship

Connect with women academics, researchers, industry professionals, and STEM leaders who have navigated the same journey.

💜
Community

Meet women who share your curiosity and ambition. Socials, general bodies, orientations — because no one should navigate STEM alone.

🌍
Outreach

We travel beyond the LUMS campus to inspire high-school girls across Pakistan to imagine themselves as scientists, engineers, and researchers.

✦
Representation

Spotlights, interviews, publications, and celebrations of women already doing incredible work in STEM — because visibility matters.

✦ FEATURED INITIATIVE — 2026

Research has no gender.
Access should have no barriers.

The All-Women National Research Article Competition 2026 was organized by LWiSE in collaboration with SBASSE, LUMS, and The Particle Magazine — opening pathways for young women across Pakistan to engage with scientific research and academic writing.

Instead of simply telling women to pursue research, we created a pathway: Learn → Write → Receive Feedback → Compete → Get Recognized → Get Published.

The competition featured an all-female panel of STEM professionals and included research-writing training for all participants — making it an educational initiative, not merely a contest.
